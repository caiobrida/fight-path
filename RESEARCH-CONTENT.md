# Learning content and media research

Current override, November 17, 2026: the owner approved SuperCombo as a preferred general/character source and waived the independent gameplay/clip publication gate for its Ken combos. FP-42 and `BNB-REVIEW.md` describe the source-backed library. Earlier proposed gates below are historical. Do not automate SF6 or claim unperformed gameplay verification.

Research date: November 17, 2026. Scope: planning only for Street Fighter 6, Ken, Classic controls, English and Brazilian Portuguese. This report proposes curriculum and editorial work; it does not approve implementation or publish game facts in the application.

## Findings and evidence limits

FightPath currently teaches three universal motions and three Ken specials. These are input exercises, not a curriculum for playing matches. The next learning layer should connect an explanation, a visible example, a short decision exercise, and a drill to perform in Street Fighter 6. A browser input match must never be presented as proof that a combo connected in the game.

The official Ken character guide supports a broad introduction to Ken as an offensive character whose kicks control space and who can pressure opponents. It was published on May 18, 2023, so it cannot establish current frame advantage, damage, invulnerability, or the best combo after later balance updates. [Official Ken guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1SX2m7sqbtA).

The Capcom command list, Buckler battle-change pages, and SuperCombo Ken pages returned HTTP 403 through the web research tool. The parent investigation successfully opened and visually inspected the [official Ken character page](https://www.streetfighter.com/6/character/ken) in the in-app browser, so this is a tool-specific access limit rather than proof the sites are unavailable. No current patch number or exact BnB route was verified in this study. Do not label a combo “current patch verified” based on this report. The existing three Ken inputs retain the separately recorded command-list verification in `SOURCES.md`.

## Proposed curriculum

These are proposed FightPath lesson designs, not sourced game facts. Each lesson should have bilingual copy, prerequisites, one learning objective, a concise explanation, a captioned example, a common mistake, a small check, and a practical drill. Terminology should preserve familiar FGC names while explaining them in Portuguese.

| Order | Learning area | Suggested lesson topics | Practical outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the game | Direction and button notation; facing direction; normals, specials and supers; hit versus block; link versus cancel | Read a move instruction and understand what the exercise measures |
| 2 | Survive and take a turn | Blocking, throws, jumps, anti-airs, punish opportunities; startup/recovery as concepts before numbers | Pick a simple defensive response and recognize an obvious punish situation |
| 3 | Fundamentals and footsies | Neutral versus pressure; move range; poke, whiff and whiff punish; backing into the corner; adjusting to an opponent's repeated choice | Explain the reason for standing at a range and choosing an action |
| 4 | SF6 systems | Drive and super resources, Drive Impact, Parry, Rush, Reversal and Burnout | Recognize resources and choose a relevant training-mode drill; exact system values require current official verification |
| 5 | Playing Ken | Broad offensive plan; chosen pokes and anti-air; moving into range; confirming; pressure versus stopping; corner decisions | Connect a small verified toolkit to a match plan instead of memorizing all moves |
| 6 | Ken BnBs | Start with a small dependable set for distinct situations, then add meter and corner variants | Choose a route because of its starter, position, available resources and purpose |
| 7 | Practice habits and adaptation | One objective per session; short focused sets; identify a repeated mistake in a replay; test a response; rest and return | Record a learning goal and one observation, without making rank or win rate the sole success measure |

Areas 1-3 and 7 explain broadly transferable concepts with examples; area 4 must be labeled Street Fighter 6, and areas 5-6 must be labeled Ken Classic. Do not imply that Drive mechanics, defensive options, cancel rules or any individual move apply unchanged across fighting games. Reading and quiz completion can acknowledge learning activity, but cannot certify match competence or rank readiness.

For example, the footsies lesson can show two fighters' ranges, ask which action is likely to miss, then send the learner to a recorded dummy drill. This would be a decision exercise, not a claim that the browser reproduces SF6 hitboxes. “Always jump less” and “always attack with Ken” should be replaced with context, risk and examples. Successful aggression includes recognizing when a turn has ended.

## Proposed first Ken BnB set

The six entries below are editorial slots to validate, not verified combo recipes. No exact route, damage number, frame value or success guarantee is supplied because the current-patch evidence was unavailable. That verification is concrete future task scope, not a reason to fill the page from memory.

| Slot | Situation and teaching goal | Required evidence before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K-BNB-01 | Close light starter: a dependable short conversion and awareness of hit versus block | Current Classic route, reach limits, hit/block dummy behavior, both facing directions |
| K-BNB-02 | Large punish: an easy meterless conversion after an obviously unsafe attack | Verified starter and cancel sequence; normal/counter/punish-counter condition stated |
| K-BNB-03 | Jump-in conversion: connect an aerial opening to a grounded route | Jump spacing, grounded timing and whether the same route works on crouching opponents |
| K-BNB-04 | Grounded poke conversion with Drive Rush: distinguish a range tool from a close starter | Verified resource cost, cancel eligibility, hit conditions and reduced-resource alternative |
| K-BNB-05 | Corner conversion: explain what changes near the wall | Explicit corner requirement, spacing, juggle restrictions and fallback outside the corner |
| K-BNB-06 | Super ender: finish a verified staple when the meter and situation justify it | Super level, cancel point, cost, game-build evidence and alternative without super |

For every published route, record the actual game build, verification date, source URL or source scene, Classic notation with a legend, both left/right presentations, starter/hit state, position, resource requirements, a simple alternative, and a reproducible training-mode recording. If damage is displayed, record the exact starting conditions and scaling. Do not introduce lengthy run-stop or character-specific optimizations before the dependable set works for beginners.

The first BnB feature can be a readable library with steps, video and a drill to do in SF6. Multi-step input rehearsal is a separate later implementation and requires a new evaluator contract; the existing single-motion evaluator cannot validate links, cancels, hits, juggle state, hit confirms or real match success.

## Media candidates and handling

1. Use the official Ken guide above as a video candidate, linked or embedded through YouTube after confirming that embedding remains enabled. Use original captions and a bilingual explanation for the selected scene; the source language need not match the interface if the lesson remains fully understandable.
2. Capture original instructional SF6 examples for the six BnB slots, range examples and anti-air examples after a verified game build is chosen. Include annotations, inputs and the relevant condition rather than using decorative unlabelled loops.
3. Create original notation diagrams and range illustrations within the product's design system. They can explain an idea without copying site graphics or implying accurate hitboxes.
4. Treat official character art and SuperCombo animation files as candidates requiring provenance and reuse review. Public visibility is not evidence of permission to redistribute a raw image or GIF. Do not hotlink files or download third-party clips merely because they appear in search results.

Capcom's [Video Policy](https://press.capcom.com/Video-Policy) permits certain instructional and transformative gameplay videos subject to its conditions; it also restricts commercialization and redistribution of extracted assets and official event footage. This does not grant a blanket license for character art or another creator's video. Its page says last updated January 6, 2021, with FAQ March 15, 2021. The [regional policy entry point](https://www.capcom-games.com/en/video-policy/) returned 403, so recheck the applicable terms and future business model before shipping assets. This report is an asset-selection recommendation, not legal clearance.

For embeds, [YouTube's player documentation](https://developers.google.com/youtube/player_parameters) supports start/end parameters and captions. Automatic playback initiates data sharing on page load. Prefer a locally rendered poster and explicit play action, no autoplay, a fallback source link, and an accompanying transcript. A privacy-enhanced host alone should not be treated as consent or as a promise of zero third-party data collection; [YouTube's developer policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies) describe data exchanged by embedded players. Accessibility and privacy review belong in the media task.

For local short demonstrations, evaluate compressed MP4/WebM clips instead of large animated GIFs; provide a poster, pause/play, reduced-motion handling, subtitles or transcript, dimensions to avoid layout shift, and a mobile transfer budget. This is a proposed implementation direction, not a library or codec decision already approved.
